# Public-source reconnaissance

Checked 2026-10-24. These are source findings and coverage notes, not field observations or a methodology. Source files, checksums, retrieval status, and license notes are in `data/source_inventory.csv`.

## Kerala Data Portal

- Inspected the catalogue and transport sector page, then traversed its 16 catalogue pages. Page 12 contained two road-related charts; its HTML body is saved as `kerala_datahub_datasets_page12.html`. Two CSV files in this folder reproduce their embedded chart JSON for offline inspection (the portal presents an in-page CSV export button, rather than a linked CSV URL).
- `districtwise_road_length.csv`: 14 districts, one selected metric, State Highways (km), source label PWD (Roads). Kottayam is listed at **340.04 km**. This is district network context, not road lengths near study crossings.
- `districtwise_motor_vehicle_growth.csv`: 14 districts, one selected series, **2024-25**, source label Motor Vehicles Department. Kottayam is listed at **1,061,662**. This is a district aggregate; it is not a count of vehicles passing a crossing or a pedestrian exposure measure. The chart title calls it districtwise growth, but the displayed selected series is labeled only 2024-25; do not infer a growth rate or unit from the value alone.
- These are the only relevant structured transport resources found during catalogue traversal; they do not evidence pedestrian demand, crossing vehicle volumes, accessibility deficiencies, or control/visibility conditions. Portal states that material may be reproduced free if accurate and prominently attributed to “Kerala Data Portal, Government of Kerala.”

## NATPAC

- Official NATPAC technical-report listing page was retrieved and saved. It lists Road Safety Policy (modified), Road Safety Action Plan, “Road Safety in Kerala—Contributing Factors and Solutions,” “Strategic Road Map for Crash Reduction … 2030,” and other studies, but links no report PDFs on that listing. Do not treat these titles as reviewed report contents.
- NATPAC's Traffic Engineering & Safety Division page describes road-safety auditing, road-user studies, crash-data analysis, blackspot identification, vulnerable-road-user work, and outreach. It says crash-vulnerable corridors on National and State Highways in Kerala were identified. Those statements establish relevant institutional scope, not local IIIT Kottayam results or a site-level dataset.
- The official Road Safety Materials page links actual PDF resources. Verified both `natpac_pedestrian_safety_booklets.pdf` and `natpac_draft_road_safety_vision.pdf`: response content-type was `application/pdf` and bodies begin `%PDF-`. The 97,986-byte booklet PDF is a two-page catalogue of booklet titles (including “Pedestrian Safety Guide”), not the full guide. The 747,213-byte Road Safety Vision PDF has **68 physical pages**, but the substantive 34-page text is repeated twice. It is a historical policy/report source whose statistics span 1960–2013. Printed p.12 discusses pedestrian safety facilities (sidewalks, zebra crossings, guardrails, beacons, and grade-separated crossings); p.13 addresses street lighting/visibility, signals, and road markings/signs. These are historical policy context and recommendations—not current Kottayam site observations. The fetch script rejects PDF claims unless both content type and magic bytes agree; HTML error pages are saved with `.html` and marked `download_not_pdf`.
- An attempted ScienceDirect PDF request for an India midblock study was HTTP 403; the attempt is retained as a failed inventory row. As a peer-reviewed fallback, fetched the open Current Science paper `peer_reviewed_india_midblock_ttc.pdf` (12 pages; verified `application/pdf` and `%PDF-`). Citation: Hareshkumar Golakiya, Ritvik Chauhan, Chintaman Santosh Bari, and Ashish Dhamaniya, “Pedestrian safety analysis at urban midblock section under mixed traffic conditions using time to collision as surrogate safety measure,” *Current Science* 123(9), 1117–1128 (10 Nov 2022), DOI [10.18520/cs/v123/i9/1117-1128](https://doi.org/10.18520/cs/v123/i9/1117-1128). The authors report video observations at four sites in Surat, Vadodara, Ahmedabad, and Jaipur, 21,110 valid pedestrian-vehicle interactions, and a study-specific TTC clustering threshold of 3.60 seconds. Factors explored include pedestrian crossing and vehicle speeds, vehicle category, gender, group size, and vehicle-pass-first/pedestrian-pass-first interactions.
- This article is useful methodological context about field video, trajectories, and surrogate safety measures—not a source for IIIT Kottayam values, pedestrian demand, treatment effect, or a transferable threshold without local validation. It does not supply local site observations. Copyright/license was not identified in the article; cite it and check publisher terms before redistribution.

## Other portals and scope limits

- India OGD state and national landing pages were fetched. They are catalogue discovery only. Government Open Data License - India is identified for published resources/metadata, but verify the individual dataset terms and ownership.
- The official IIIT Kottayam landing page was fetched for institutional context only; no crossing attributes or counts were extracted.
- OSM/Nominatim files provide campus coordinates, a campus bbox, and mapped feature geometry/tags within the recorded campus-centered extract. These numeric geospatial inputs inform explicitly labeled synthetic geographic proxies in `scripts/build_synthetic_dataset.py`; they are not measured demand, traffic exposure, accessibility/control ratings, or field observations. The extract is not district-wide, and OSM tags do not establish crossing usability. The OSM inventory description is regenerated from current metadata and hashes the current raw files.
- No fetched source supplies verified site-level pedestrian counts, vehicle counts, or field condition ratings. Aggregate district/state statistics and historical policy statistics must not substitute for those measurements or be treated as actual crossing scores. The inventory preserves independently contributed rows whose IDs are not managed by this script.

Refresh portal/NATPAC/research-paper bodies with `python scripts/fetch_public_sources.py`. After an optional OSM refresh, rebuild the inventory without network requests using `python scripts/fetch_public_sources.py --cached`. This mode recalculates hashes from the current raw snapshot and updates OSM/Nominatim coverage and fields from current metadata; it preserves other inventory rows whose IDs are not managed by this script.
