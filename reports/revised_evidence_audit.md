Read-only audit of the supplied submission guidance, the current report text, generator, evaluator, model, saved data, source inventory, and evaluation summary. No project outputs were changed. Numerical spot-checks below are from the checked-in evaluation summary and implementation; a fresh Python rerun was not possible here because pandas is unavailable.

## Guidance implications

- The required report length is about four to five pages, excluding references and appendices. The guidance requires at least three closely related papers plus a reliable method reference, a precise question, criteria/source justification, uncertainty derivation, one complete worked example, baseline(s), sensitivity and explicit validation limits.
- Its simple-priority and no-extension baselines are already covered in principle by the four-criterion model and the central-score crisp average. The latter is a normalized arithmetic-average comparator, not crisp TOPSIS. The sample separately suggests a barrier-only comparison and a crisp TOPSIS worked baseline. Neither is currently present for the 28-row synthetic input. Consider computing these from the current CSV only if they add a useful diagnostic and label them as further synthetic method comparisons. Do not replace or silently alter current outputs. The four-row historical demo already reports crisp ranks, but it is a distinct invented dataset, not a crisp TOPSIS analysis of the main input.
- The guidance explicitly prohibits inventing observations, experts, intervention effects, validation, or authorship. It asks for genuine team contribution information; no author names or contributions can be inferred from this repository or the account. Keep identity details for the team to supply, or omit from an internal draft, not fabricate them.

## Supported implementation and numerical facts

- The main data contain 28 synthetic alternatives, selected as 27 mapped-road-junction candidates and one mapped crossing, all unverified. The source methodology also permits destination-near-road candidates, but none occur in these 28 selected rows. “Crossings” should not imply a verified inventory. The main candidates use a deterministic 3 km radius, target 28, and at least 80 m spacing. The saved extract has 431 elements, 286 accepted motor-road ways and 91 deduplicated destination objects.
- The saved OSM/Nominatim materials support map coordinates, mapped tags, geometry, and the listed contextual objects only. The source register says the Overpass request completion time was not captured. `2026-10-24T12:41:47Z` is archival verification time; `2026-10-24T12:38:18Z` is the OSM base timestamp, not HTTP retrieval time. Say “snapshot archived/verified 24 October 2026” rather than claiming that is the request/download date. Nominatim completion time likewise was not captured.
- The snapshot is older than the attempted wider-buffer/member-node query, which did not complete. Road-member child-node tags are not guaranteed. The flags say all 28 selected points have their 500 m context disk within the saved rectangle, but this does not establish map completeness. In particular, 27 have no recognized candidate-node access/control evidence; their deficiency scores are mostly assumption-based. Avoid suggesting mapped absence or comprehensive tag coverage.
- All five main criteria are generated as ordinal integer centers 1–5 with fuzzy triples clipped to that scale. `round_clip(x) = min(5,max(1,floor(x+0.5)))`. Their score rules are: vehicle uses the highest hierarchy adjacent road (tie broken by lower way ID), constants 5/4/3/2/1 by road class, plus 0.5 for explicitly numeric lanes >=3 and 0.5 for numeric maxspeed >=60, then seeded U(-0.35,0.35); pedestrian demand uses 1 + 1.05 ln(1+I) + 0.35B + 0.12(vehicle score−2) + U(-0.65,0.65); E1 destination uses 1 + 1.05M + 0.25 ln(1+I); access deficiency uses prior 3 + shared U(-0.45,0.45) + N(0,0.85), adjusted only by explicitly mapped wheelchair, kerb/sloped curb and tactile tags; control/visibility deficiency uses prior 3 + same shared variation + 0.2(vehicle−3) + independent N(0,0.85), adjusted by explicitly mapped signals/markings and lighting. All are rounded/clipped. These are model rules, not measured scales. The generator seed is 20261024; sensitivity seed is 20261009.
- `I` sums weighted mapped-destination contributions `a(1-d/600)` for destinations within 500 m, and `M` is the strongest contribution; `B` indicates mapped bus presence within 250 m. Distances are straight-line. These formulas do not measure pedestrian counts, walking routes, access necessity, or demand. The demand and E1 scores share amenity inputs by design; their central-score Spearman correlation is 0.800621. This is a construction diagnostic, not independent empirical association.
- Widths are synthetic heuristics: ±1 for low-confidence proxies and ±1.5 for very-low/unknown assumptions, clipped to 1–5. They are neither confidence intervals nor calibrated membership functions. Integer ordinal steps are treated as equally spaced by TOPSIS, an explicit approximation.
- The report's listed leading scores and ranks agree with `reports/evaluation/summary.json`. The 5-criterion top is an exact tie at 0.6839195867 between SYN-OSM-716872373 and SYN-OSM-764885861. Against the four-criterion fuzzy baseline, top-three overlap is 2/3 and Spearman is 0.908568; top-1 does not change. The recorded 1,000 weight perturbations have min/mean top-three overlap 1.000 and min/mean Spearman 0.9641/0.9897; only the two tied candidates are selected top by the evaluator's deterministic ID ordering, 514 and 486 times. These are scenario frequencies, not probabilities. Width scenarios retain the same tied leaders; Spearman versus full width is 0.9766 and 0.9845 for factors 0 and 0.5.
- The current crisp comparator independently min-max normalizes central scores for the first four criteria and averages active columns; it is not TOPSIS. The report says so accurately. Evaluation of the main data does not currently provide the sample's barrier-only ranking or central-value crisp TOPSIS. Do not label the crisp comparator as TOPSIS.
- Model arithmetic: common criterion bounds are min low and max high, triangular components normalized to [0,1]; non-discriminating columns are dropped and equal weights renormalized. Weighted triangles use theoretical ideals `(w,w,w)` and `(0,0,0)`. Chen vertex distances are summed across criteria and closeness is `D-/(D+ + D-)`. This is not an unchanged Chen implementation: Chen's original method addresses group decision-making with fuzzy ratings and fuzzy criterion weights, and its normalization/ideal construction differ. Accurate description is “Chen-style vertex distance and TOPSIS closeness, with project-specific common-bound normalization, crisp equal weights, theoretical weighted ideals, active-column handling, and summed distances.” The existing model docstring correctly calls these adaptations.
- The Appendix B five-criterion example matches the stored trace: for SYN-OSM-716872373, D+=0.3529555111, D-=0.7637081485 and C=0.6839195867. The older four-row demo is distinct, invented, and uses separate units/scales; its main rank reversal HYP-C from 4th to 1st is correctly listed. It must remain explicitly a teaching illustration, not evidence about the 28-candidate experiment.

## Specific cautions for a natural revision

- Keep the source document's original title exactly: “A Fuzzy TOPSIS Framework for Prioritising Pedestrian Crossing Safety and Accessibility Improvements.” The document currently contains placeholders for team names, department and emails. No names are known from this audit; final submission needs genuine team information or must follow the instructor's format without invented identities.
- Avoid “public map data informs scores” without immediately distinguishing mapped facts, formula-derived indicators, and seeded synthetic values. Do not characterize the whole dataset as five observed criteria.
- The output's `feasibility_status=eligible` means included in simulation only. No actual feasible packages, field observations, photographs, user routes, costs, or independent review exist.
- A five-versus-four comparison holds the other model setup constant and adds E1 at equal weight (weights renormalize from 1/4 to 1/5). It measures a constructed mathematical consequence, not improved decision quality. E1 itself is not the field definition requiring verified walking routes and necessity.
- Keep the exact-tie qualification prominent. Scores are candidate-set-dependent and not comparable across the 28-row and four-row datasets. Near ties and deterministic ID tie-break ordering are not meaningful evidence of distinct site priority.
- No actual project validation is reported. Unit tests and repeatability establish computational consistency only. Say explicitly that the guidance's empirical/independent validation deliverable remains unmet, rather than recasting sensitivity or model agreement as validation.
- There are no confirmed author names, author affiliation details beyond IIIT Kottayam indicated in supplied report, email addresses, or contribution records in the inspected material. Do not infer personal names from account identity.
